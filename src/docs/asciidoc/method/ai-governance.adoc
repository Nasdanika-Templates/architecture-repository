[#ai-governance]
= AI Governance Mappings

NOTE: This chapter was drafted with an AI assistant and has not been reviewed by an AI governance,
legal or measurement specialist. It shows where this content goes in the repository, not what any
framework or regulation requires. Check it with your own specialists.

An AI system needs the evidence of AI governance: what the system is for, what it may not do, which
risks were identified, how they are mitigated and measured, who accepted the rest, and how the
system is watched once it is live. A repository that is versioned, reviewed and attributed is a
natural home for that evidence, and an auditor can be given a PDF of a tagged version.

[#ai-frameworks]
== Frameworks and Regulations

Which frameworks apply depends on the organization and the jurisdiction. The ones most often
relevant, and where they touch the document:

NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)::
Its four functions, _Govern_, _Map_, _Measure_ and _Manage_, are a useful outline for <<ai-risk>>:
the governance and roles, the context and risks, the evaluations and metrics, and the treatment of
risks. Its Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1) lists risks specific to generative AI, such as
confabulation and information security, which make a good checklist.

ISO/IEC 42001::
The management system standard for AI. If the organization is certified or intends to be, the
document may support the system-level controls, such as impact assessment, data, documentation and
monitoring. Ask whoever owns the certification.

EU AI Act::
Classifies AI systems by risk. The sample proposes that a customer-facing chat assistant that informs
and does not decide carries transparency obligations, and that evaluating creditworthiness would make
it high-risk. Ask your legal and compliance functions to confirm the classification of your system,
and record it and its reasoning as a decision.

Model risk management in banking::
Banking regulators expect models to be inventoried, validated independently and monitored, for
example under the US supervisory guidance SR 11-7 or its local equivalents. Whether a generative
AI system is a "model" in that sense is itself a question for the model risk function: record it as
an open question until they answer.

OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications::
The security risks of LLM applications, such as prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure
and excessive agency. Use it as the checklist for the threat model.

[#governance-in-repository]
== What the Repository Adds

* *Traceability.* Each risk links to the decisions that address it and to the evaluation that
  measures it; each decision to the meeting where it was made.
* *Accountability.* Only people move decisions to DECIDED; the commit shows who.
* *Point-in-time evidence.* A tag rebuilds the exact document a review approved.
* *The AI that writes the document is governed too.* The assistant drafts and people review, under
  the same principles. Check that your assistant is approved for the data in the repository before
  you give it the repository; a bank's architecture documents are confidential.
