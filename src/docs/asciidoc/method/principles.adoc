[#principles]
= Principles

Architecture work produces a lot of text: context, options, trade-offs, decisions, assumptions,
open questions, notes from meetings. An AI assistant is good at producing and reorganizing text,
and poor at being accountable for it. The method assigns the work accordingly. It rests on four
principles.

[#ai-drafts-people-decide]
== AI Drafts, People Decide

The assistant drafts: a section from a prompt, an AsciiDoc page from a meeting transcript, a list of
alternatives with criteria and a ranking, a glossary entry, a review of the document for
inconsistencies. People decide: which alternative, which risk to accept, what is in scope, whether
the draft is right.

Two consequences follow.

* A ranking produced by the assistant is an input to a decision, never the decision. In the sample,
  the assistant ranks the chat models (<<chat-models>>), and the decision stays
  [.pending]#PENDING# until an evaluation on the bank's own data and a named owner close it.
* Every change the assistant makes is reviewed before it is committed, as a diff
  (<<review-changes>>). The commit is made by a person, who owns it.

[#everything-is-text]
== Everything Is Text, Under Version Control

The sources of the document are text: AsciiDoc for prose and tables, PlantUML for diagrams, Markdown
for prompts, YAML for the theme. Meeting transcripts and notes are text files in the repository.
External information, such as the status of Jira issues, is pulled into text files at build time
(<<external-information>>).

Text is what an assistant reads and writes best, what Git diffs and merges, and what survives tool
changes. A diagram drawn as code can be reviewed line by line; a diagram pasted as a picture cannot.

Version control gives the rest for free: who changed what and when, why (the commit message), the
state of the architecture at any past date (a tag or a commit), parallel proposals (branches) and
a review process (pull requests).

[#regenerate-dont-patch]
== Regenerate, Don't Patch

When an input changes, regenerate what depends on it; do not patch the output by hand and hope the
rest still agrees.

* *Generated content stays generated.* Content produced by a tool, such as a Jira snapshot or the
  documentation of a model, is rebuilt from its source, never edited. The fix goes into the source
  or the generator.
* *Drafted content is redrafted from its inputs.* When a meeting changes the scope, give the
  assistant the new meeting note and ask it to bring every affected section in line, then review
  the diff. Patching one paragraph leaves the summary, the context diagram, the alternatives and the
  glossary telling the old story.
* *Prompts are inputs too.* Keep the prompts that shaped the document in `prompts/`, so a section
  can be regenerated, and its rationale reconstructed, later.

Regeneration is cheap with an assistant and safe with Git: the diff shows exactly what moved.

[#separate-fact-assumption-decision]
== Separate Fact, Assumption, and Decision

Most architecture documents blur three kinds of statements, and the blur is where projects get
hurt: an assumption reads like a fact and is never validated, or a preference reads like a decision
nobody made.

[horizontal]
Fact:: Has a source that a reader can check.
Assumption:: Has an owner, a way to validate it and a date by which it must be validated. Lives in
<<assumptions>>.
Decision:: Has an owner, a status, the alternatives considered and the reason. Lives in
<<decision-log>>.

Everything else is an open question (<<open-questions>>). Ask the assistant to classify statements
when it converts a meeting note, and to flag any that it cannot classify.
