[#baselines]
= Organization Baselines

A cloud provider's public catalog is not what an organization may use. Organizations use a subset:
the regions that meet their data residency rules, the virtual machine sizes their landing zone
allows, the services their cloud governance has approved, the AI models and versions on their
approved list, the deployment types, tiers and quotas they have bought. On top of the cloud are
the organization's own platforms, such as a container platform, an API gateway or a monitoring
stack, each with its own onboarding rules and lead times.

An architecture that assumes the public catalog can be right on paper and impossible to deploy.
An AI assistant makes this more likely, not less: it knows the public catalog as of its training,
which is both broader than the organization's subset and out of date.

[#confirm-and-baseline]
== Confirm, then Baseline

Organization-specific offerings are confirmed and baselined.

Confirmed::
With the owner of the subset: the cloud platform team, the cloud center of excellence, Information
Security, the AI governance function. Until it is confirmed, an offering the architecture relies on
is an [.assumption]#ASSUMPTION# in <<assumptions>>, with the owner who can confirm it; once
confirmed, it is a [.fact]#FACT# with its source (<<separate-fact-assumption-decision>>).

Baselined::
Recorded in the repository with its as-of date and its source, so that the architecture states
which version of the subset it was designed against. When the subset changes, a model version
retired, a region added, a service approved, the baseline changes in a reviewed commit, and the
diff shows what in the architecture depends on the change.

The sample's baseline is in <<platform-baseline>>. It is a table written by hand, which is where to
start. When the subset is published by a system, such as the allowed locations and SKUs in the
cloud's policy definitions or the AI governance function's model register, the baseline can be a
snapshot pulled at build time (<<external-information>>), and a check in the build can fail when an
evaluation or an ADR relies on something outside it.

[#baselines-and-evaluations]
== Baselines and Evaluations

An alternative that is outside the baseline is not ruled out, but it carries a cost the evaluation
must show: an exception to request, a service to onboard, a delay. The sample's evaluations score
this as a criterion, availability within the baseline, so that an alternative that needs an
exception ranks lower for that reason, visibly, rather than being dropped without a trace or
recommended without a warning (<<embedding-models>>, <<adr-001>>).
